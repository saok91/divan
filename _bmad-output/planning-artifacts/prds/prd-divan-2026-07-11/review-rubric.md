# PRD Quality Review — Divan HRIS

## Overall verdict
This is a disciplined, unusually substantive product-scope contract with a clear thesis, coherent capability model, explicit non-goals, strong temporal/headcount integrity, and honest launch gates. It is not yet launch-grade as an executable acceptance contract: several product semantics and control artifacts remain unapproved, material NFR bounds and outcome targets are unset, and one data-governance statement contradicts the launch gates; the PRD is therefore fit to govern decision closure and architecture discovery, but not yet to authorize build completion or production launch.

## Decision-readiness — thin
The PRD consistently states decisions as requirements and exposes rather than conceals uncertainty. The conservative future-capacity rule is explicit in FR-30, non-goals are direct, and §14 assigns owners to unresolved matters. However, the remaining decisions include the semantics that determine whether core event history, access, migration, headcount, and operations are correct; a decision-maker can authorize closure work, but cannot yet approve an executable launch baseline.

### Findings
- **high** Core correctness remains behind an unclosed decision package (§14.1–§14.10) — The open gates include the “field/category × action × Organizational Scope matrix,” “exact Effective Date/timezone/end-boundary,” headcount matching and expiry, migration atomicity, LDAP outage behavior, RPO/RTO, performance/load profile, and metric catalogue. These are not peripheral implementation choices: they change permission outcomes, event resolution, capacity outcomes, acceptance evidence, and operational safety. *Fix:* Close each gate into a named, versioned, owner-approved policy or acceptance artifact; reference its version from the affected FR/NFR and make approval a prerequisite to the relevant architecture and story baseline, not only to go-live.
- **high** Data ownership is required both before and after launch (§5.2 FR-7, §8.1 LG-3, §10) — FR-7 requires every enabled field to have an approved “owner/source,” and LG-3 blocks launch pending Data Dictionary approval, while §10 says “Data owner and authoritative source must be identified for each enabled field after go-live.” The latter permits the system to launch without a control the former sections require. *Fix:* Change “after go-live” to “before enablement and before production launch,” and state how ownership changes are governed thereafter.
- **medium** The authoritative PRD states the reservation choice but not its accepted cost (§5.7 FR-30; addendum §5.3) — FR-30 mandates immediate reservation, while only the non-authoritative addendum explains that it “prevents overbooking but can reduce near-term apparent availability” and still says Workforce Planning “must validate it.” The requirement therefore looks settled while owner acceptance of its operational trade-off remains unresolved. *Fix:* Put the trade-off and approving owner in the PRD, and either mark the rule as an approved decision or explicitly gate FR-30 baseline approval on Workforce Planning sign-off.

## Substance over theater — adequate
Most detail is earned. The five named journeys drive temporal behavior, migration, scoped reporting, and privileged operations; the NFRs are specific to an on-premise, sensitive-data HRIS rather than generic “secure/scalable” furniture; and the addendum clearly labels candidate inventories and implementation options as non-authoritative. The weak spot is repeated use of “approved” as a stand-in for contracts that do not yet exist, which creates local impressions of testability that the document as a whole has not earned.

### Findings
- **medium** Approval placeholders obscure which acceptance contract applies (§5.1 FR-1 and FR-4; §5.3 FR-13; §5.4 FR-19; §5.8 FR-36; §5.9 FR-41) — Phrases such as “approved identity policy,” “approved management relationships,” “under an approved policy,” “approved launch report catalogue,” and “approved launch format” recur without stable artifact names or versions. Owners are often named later, but downstream teams cannot tell which exact policy closes which consequence. *Fix:* Give each cross-cutting artifact a canonical name/ID, map affected FR consequences to it, and record approval state/version in a compact dependencies or launch-artifacts register.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis in §1.4—“governed migration plus effective-dated, immutable personnel events, deterministic historical reconstruction, permission-scoped reporting, and headcount control”—clearly drives the capability groups, launch gates, measured outcomes, and counter-metrics. The scope is a coherent platform-style MVP rather than a disguised backlog, but success remains observational rather than decisional until targets exist.

### Findings
- **high** Success metrics cannot yet confirm or falsify the thesis (§8 opening; §8.2 SM-1–SM-6) — The PRD says owners “MUST publish numeric 30/60/90-day targets before production launch,” while every SM currently says only “baseline and monitor.” The measures align well to trusted state and spreadsheet retirement, but no threshold defines acceptable trust, migration quality, authoritative completion, spreadsheet reduction, task improvement, Override health, or meaningful adoption. *Fix:* Add owner-approved target values, minimum launch/30/60/90 success thresholds, and remediation or stop conditions to the metric catalogue, then reference its approved version from §8 before calling the PRD launch-grade.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
The FR structure is strong: all 45 FRs have explicit consequences, many state atomicity, invariants, failure outcomes, and permission boundaries, and the addendum supplies a useful candidate test inventory. Done-ness nevertheless breaks down wherever owner-approved policies or quantitative bounds are still absent; an engineer can design the system, but acceptance cannot determine completion for several launch-critical qualities and one deliberately fuzzy capability boundary.

### Findings
- **high** Launch-critical NFRs lack measurable acceptance bounds (§9 NFR-2, NFR-3, NFR-5–NFR-13) — NFR-2 labels its 2/3/5-second values “proposed—not yet confirmed MUST/p95 gates”; processing freshness, availability, RPO/RTO, sessions, retention, audit, support, accessibility, and scale similarly defer exact values or profiles. Marking absence as launch-blocking is honest, but does not tell engineering or acceptance what “done” means and can force architecture rework after implementation. *Fix:* Approve a versioned NFR acceptance profile with numeric thresholds, workload/environment bounds, evidence methods, and responsible signers before architecture and delivery baselines are accepted.
- **medium** Secondary Assignment MVP scope is not enumerable (§5.3 FR-12; §7.1–§7.2) — “Only to the extent required to preserve and maintain current organizational data already used by the organization” and “broader Secondary Assignment workflows” do not identify which create/change/end cases, fields, reports, imports, or conflicts are required. Two teams can implement materially different scopes and both claim compliance. *Fix:* Add an approved finite Secondary Assignment scenario/data inventory, with explicit included operations and non-goals, and bind FR-12 acceptance to it.

## Scope honesty — strong
The PRD is explicit about MVP, deferred work, and non-goals; it does not smuggle payroll, employee self-service, generic workflow, mobile, multi-tenancy, or architecture choices into launch scope. Unknowns are gathered in §14 with owners and are described as launch gates, while §15 transparently states why no inline assumptions are used. The high open-item density is a readiness problem, not hidden scope.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The glossary is extensive and materially used, FR-1–FR-45 and UJ-1–UJ-5 are contiguous and unique, journeys have named protagonists, and sections can generally be extracted independently. Architecture, UX, and test planning can proceed from the stable invariants, but story acceptance and final architecture baselines remain conditional on the unresolved policy, metric, Data Dictionary, and NFR artifacts identified above.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape fits a chain-top, multi-stakeholder enterprise HRIS: concise journeys establish the load-bearing operational flows, capabilities carry the detailed contract, cross-cutting security/data/operations concerns have dedicated treatment, and implementation mechanics are separated into a clearly non-authoritative addendum. The document is neither persona-heavy nor forced into a consumer-product template, and its detail level is warranted by migration, temporal, privacy, access, and headcount risk.

## Mechanical notes
- FR IDs are contiguous and unique from FR-1 through FR-45; UJ IDs are contiguous from UJ-1 through UJ-5. SM-1–SM-6 and SM-C1–SM-C5 use a clear separate counter-metric namespace.
- No unresolved ID cross-reference or broken file cross-reference was found in the reviewed PRD/addendum.
- The Assumptions Index roundtrip is internally consistent: the PRD declares “No inline assumptions are used,” and no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag appears.
- All UJs have named protagonists carrying role context inline.
- Glossary usage is consistent overall. “Cancel/Reverse/Correction” sometimes names actions while “Cancelled/Reversed/Corrected” names reportable states; the distinction is understandable but should be fixed as explicit status vocabulary in the eventual event-policy artifact.
